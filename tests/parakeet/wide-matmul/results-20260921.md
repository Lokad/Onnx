# Wide encoder MatMul: correctness passes, timing attribution rejected

The isolated reduction-block prototypes preserve every tested output bit, but
this first timing grid cannot establish a kernel gain. Its identical fallback
controls differ by **4.8–9.2%** after averaging four processes. Keep the complete
observations and correct the measurement design before selecting a product change.

## Actual operands and correctness

Four complete encoder requests from the qualified Parakeet trace provide frame
counts 51, 106, 167 and 225. Six declared diagnostic outputs retain operands and
results for layer-zero feed-forward expansion, contraction and attention Q
projection. All complete encoder outputs remain byte-identical to the original
Memory-policy trace. This uses unchanged Core `d1f86a73`, CPU 2 and normal
.NET 10.0.12 on Windows i7-14700KF.

The twelve fixtures have standard row-major layouts with constant B shapes
`[1024,4096]`, `[4096,1024]` and `[1024,1024]`. The auditor independently checks
their 27 arrays and verifies all three weights directly against the original
ONNX external-data ranges, after validating the complete asset digests.

The prototype visits reduction blocks of 128, 256 or 512 in ascending order,
retaining each output's original FMA chain. It accepts full 32-column panels
and uses the original single-row remainder. The existing six-row packed kernel
is a separate comparator. In each of four fresh processes, all 144 distinct
synthetic geometries pass nonzero-accumulator and output-canary comparisons for
all four variants. All real-fixture outputs also pass bitwise checks after every
call; caller operands and packed buffers remain unchanged.

This is kernel and diagnostic correctness evidence. It does not qualify a new
product dispatcher or repair the existing native duration-logit discrepancies.

## Why the timings are not attributed to the kernel

Each process runs public MatMul, packing alone, five prepared consumers and
five packing-plus-consumer roles. The combined timer includes clearing, pool
rental, packing, multiplication and return. All combined roles at M=51 execute
the **same public fallback**. Their aggregate maximum/minimum ratios are:

| M | B shape | Identical-role maximum / minimum |
|---:|---|---:|
| 51 | `[1024,4096]` | 1.0692 |
| 51 | `[4096,1024]` | 1.0924 |
| 51 | `[1024,1024]` | 1.0477 |

The grid retains 36,066 warmup and 4,608 measured calls. Warmup uses at least
32 calls and 0.25 elapsed seconds per role, followed by eight measured passes.
Rotating a fixed role order balances some positions but leaves each role with
mostly the same preceding workload. In particular, the baseline combined role
usually follows a prepared consumer, while other combined roles usually follow
row-major work. Different cache states are a plausible confound; cache misses
were not measured. A large baseline outlier also remains in the 106-row square
case. No samples or workers are discarded.

The next comparison must condition each role immediately before measurement,
balance preceding roles, and check the unchanged M=51 controls before accepting
any gain. Full 225-row cases remain visible even if a later dispatch hypothesis
keeps their existing three-row kernel. No application, AMD or ORT speedup follows
from this grid, and production source remains unchanged.
